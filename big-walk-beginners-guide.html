First time? Read this

Big Walk is a 2-12 player co-op puzzle game with no combat, no death, and no single-player mode — almost every puzzle needs at least two players. Before you head out, get clear on three things: what kind of game this is, what to pack, and which mistakes to dodge.

The essentials

Players & co-op

Supports 2-12 players. There's no single-player mode, and most puzzles need teamwork, so solo play will leave you stuck. When you make a lobby, set it to your real player count: 2, 3, or 4+. Set it wrong and some puzzles won't even activate — and you can't change it mid-game, so you'd have to restart. Puzzles scale with player count, but the cap is 4: even with 12 players, the mechanics are sized for 4.

The 3-item carry limit

By default you can only hold one item in your hands. Find a backpack (adds a slot, fits almost anything) and a hip bag/waist bag (adds a slot, only small things like a walkie-talkie), and you can carry up to 3 items total. Anything stored in a bag can't be dropped even when you fall off a cliff. Note that a friend has to help you put them on — you can't equip them yourself.

Chat range is tiny

Voice and text chat only reach about 2-3 meters, and terrain, walls, slopes, and facing direction muffle or even block the sound. Once you split up, you're down to walkie-talkies, radios, flares, or flare guns. Long-distance communication is the real challenge here — most people get stuck because they didn't explain what they saw, not because they're bad at puzzles.

Light & day/night

Night gets really dark, and the day/night cycle can't be skipped or sped up — the darkness is deliberate. Never go out without a light: a lantern (a big warm circle), the directional flashlight, or one of the large bouncy glow balls you'll find around the island. A smart trick is to leave a glow ball at a crossroads before dark so your group can regroup after splitting.

Towers & progression

Open the Red Tower first to unlock the Map Room and grab the paper map and compass — the rest gets much easier. Then explore Green (chairlift), Yellow (tunnel), and Blue (train) freely, and finish with the Black Tower (normal ending). It's open-world, so the order is flexible, but Red first and then Green → Yellow → Blue feels smoothest.

Getting started checklist

  • 01

    Don't skip the tutorial

    The tutorial area works like a small gym — practice arm movements and learn how buttons and light boxes work, complete its four co-op puzzles, cut the key, and lower the red drawbridge to get onto the island. The first thing you get is a flashlight, which becomes your reliable directional light for nighttime exploring.

  • 02

    Pack the essentials

    A bag, a foldable map, a compass, a flare (or flare gun), and a walkie-talkie or radio. Never leave without comms, and never head out at night without a light — before dark, grab a glow ball and drop it at the meeting-point crossroads.

  • 03

    Open the Map Room first

    Once the drawbridge is down, don't wander. Prioritize the Red Tower's five puzzles, five red gourds, and key cutting, then open the blue Map Room near spawn. Inside you'll find a portable map and a compass, and navigation flips from guesswork to following coordinates. Stepping in for the first time also unlocks the Big View achievement; carry the map in a backpack, space is limited. This is the one step every guide recommends doing first.

  • 04

    Clear the towers in order

    After the Red Tower you're free. Work through Green (chairlift), Yellow (tunnel), then Blue (train), grabbing bags and radios along the way; finish with the Black Tower for the normal Big Goodbye ending. For the true ending, come back for the 7 purple puzzles and all 15 gourds.

  • 05

    Worried about dropping things? Use Lost and Found

    Whatever anyone is holding when the host ends the session turns up on the Lost and Found table at the start gym next time you play; if nobody held it, it respawns where you originally found it. So if you want to keep a certain item, have someone hold it before you log off.

    • Quick reference: useful items

    The few things the tutorial hands you won't carry you — the genuinely useful 'gear' is almost all found while exploring. Below are the items worth knowing, grouped by purpose, with how to use each one and where to find it. (Compiled from in-game experience and public guides.)

    Carry capacity

    • BackpackStorage

      Use: Carry on your back for an extra slot that holds almost anything; anything stowed can't be dropped off a cliff. A friend has to put it on for you.

      Where: Inside the giant blue basket-like buildings — 9 of them around the island, one bag each; 10 bags including waist bags in total.

    • Waist / hip bagStorage

      Use: Carry on your hip for a second slot, but small items only (walkie-talkies, handheld radios) — a perfect 'comms pocket'. A friend has to buckle it on for you.

      Where: In the same blue circular buildings as the backpacks.

    Communication & signalling

    • Walkie-talkieComms

      Use: Not limited by the 2-3 meter voice range: hold it to talk and listen, hang it on your hip to listen only. The cheap setup is one per squad — the caller holds it, the listeners wear it.

      Where: On the yellow frame below the Red Tower overlooking the ravine, by the red tunnel near the Red Tower, and on another yellow frame on a mountainside near the centre of the map (visible from the Blue Tower).

    • Handheld radioComms

      Use: Play back each radio channel you've unlocked so lost teammates can follow the sound, or use it as a distant gather-up signal.

      Where: Grab it by the first radio station on the crosswalk, just past the first music area.

    • Signal flareLocating

      Use: Press the red button to fire a flare that lights the area and shows teammates where you are — the fastest fix when you're split up.

      Where: At fixed points: down the path from the tutorial exit, beside the Map Room, and on the top of every control-panel tower.

    • Flare gunLocating

      Use: Fire a smoke flare straight up to light up an area, roughly every 20 seconds. There are four — red, green, yellow, and blue.

      Where: Four ring-shaped buildings around the map, one flare gun each.

    • MegaphoneComms

      Use: Carries your voice far beyond normal range — great for calling down from a mountaintop or tower, or coordinating from above.

      Where: Three in total, all on top of yellow cube blocks: two near the Yellow and Green Towers, one near the Purple Tunnel.

    • WhiteboardComms

      Use: Leave messages for teammates who arrive later; they're readable from a distance with binoculars. Great for async communication.

      Where: Three beside the Map Room, one behind the coordinate locator below the Green Tower.

    Navigation

    • Foldable mapNav

      Use: Check the island layout, key landmarks, and which progress flags are done, then pin specific spots using coordinates.

      Where: On the table in the Map Room (the blue building below the Red Tower — not the Blue Tower's building).

    • CompassNav

      Use: Shows the cardinal directions so you can call out 'north of so-and-so tower' — essential for giving positions over a walkie-talkie.

      Where: Sits in the Map Room alongside the foldable map.

    • GPS / coordinates trackerNav

      Use: Displays your exact coordinates in real time, so you can line up a target coordinate fast, and solves a couple of coordinate puzzles.

      Where: Inside a red, square structure below the Green Tower, near the chairlift station.

    • BinocularsNav

      Use: Zoom in on distant areas to scout before trekking, so you don't run all the way there for nothing. Pairs nicely with the foldable map.

      Where: On top of every control-panel tower.

    • Telescope (fixed)Nav

      Use: Like binoculars, but it also lights up distant puzzle targets, helping you pin exactly where to go.

      Where: On platforms around the puzzle areas.

    Light

    • LanternLight

      Use: Projects a large circle of warm amber light — the basic light source for exploring at night.

      Where: In the tutorial area, at points of interest, and scattered around the island; grab one as you pass.

    • Directional flashlightLight

      Use: Shines straight onto a specific item to see it in the dark — and doubles as a light signal (agree on a number of blinks for yes/no).

      Where: Your first one is the reward for crossing the red drawbridge; control-panel towers hold more.

    • Glow ballLight

      Use: A large bouncy orb you can roll. Roll one to a crossroads before dark as a waypoint so a split group can regroup, or use it for light signalling.

      Where: Large bouncy glow bulbs are scattered in many corners of the island.

    Other handy items

    • PaintbrushMisc

      Use: Paint on the ground, rocks, or even teammates — draw arrows and leave notes you can't say out loud.

      Where: In the customisation room.

    • Laser pointerMisc

      Use: Point at something from a distance to indicate 'we're heading here next', especially from up high. Great at night.

      Where: On top of every control-panel tower; the Map Room hides three more.

    • Item scannerMisc

      Use: Marks nearby interactable items with a yellow X, so hunting for a specific object is much faster.

      Where: By the flare point near the tutorial area, and inside every control-panel tower.

    • CowbellMisc

      Use: Call a teammate from far away with sound rather than text.

      Where: In the tutorial area.

    Why it won't work

    Can I play solo?

    No. There's no single-player mode and almost every puzzle needs at least two players, so you'll get stuck alone. There's also no matchmaking to find people — you invite friends or use a session code.

    A teammate got lost?

    Fire a flare to mark your position (they're on tower tops, outside the Map Room, and at the central mountain's peak), or call them on a walkie-talkie. Smarter still: drop a glow ball at a crossroads before dark and regroup there.

    Can't carry enough?

    You can only hold one item by default. Find a backpack and a hip bag to add a slot each — that's a 3-item maximum, and anything stowed can't be dropped off a cliff. Remember a friend has to help you put them on.

    Too dark at night?

    Bring a lantern, a flashlight, or grab a glow ball. The day/night cycle can't be skipped or sped up, so when it's dark you either tough it out with light or just wait for sunrise.

    A puzzle won't activate / nothing reacts?

    Most likely the lobby was set to the wrong player count — some puzzles need a minimum number of players, and won't trigger otherwise. Reopen the lobby at your true count instead of forcing it.

    Dropped an item or key off a cliff?

    Don't panic. Whatever anyone is holding when the host ends the session shows up on the Lost and Found table at spawn next time; anything not held respawns where you first found it.
