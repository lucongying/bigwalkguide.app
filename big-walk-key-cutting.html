Keys won't work until you cut them

Big Walk's biggest hidden mechanic: keys from puzzles don't open locks directly — you must shape them at key cutter stations, in order, running through all five cutters before a key is ready. The rule is identical in every tower — gather five collectibles, get a blank key, follow the arrows through all five cutters, then insert the finished key into the right lock. Skipping this is the #1 reason players get stuck.

Before you start

  • Solve the puzzles around the area and collect all 5 rewards (the Red Tower wants 5 red gourds; Green, Yellow and Blue each want 5 same-colored Heads)
  • Place every reward into the receiver slots yourself — solving a puzzle doesn't submit its reward, so someone has to carry it in. The blank key only drops when all five slots are lit.
  • The key is useless until you've cut it
  • Assign one permanent key carrier and one light/arrow reader, with the rest following — don't pass the key around between players
  • Don't run to the door after only a couple of cuts — you must cut all five, in order. Skipping or undercutting won't open anything.

How to solve it

  1. 01

    Collect 5 rewards and make the key drop

    Each key needs a set of collectibles. The Red Tower wants 5 red gourds; Green, Yellow and Blue each want 5 same-colored Heads. Carry them into the receiver slots yourself — the top terminal on the Red Tower, the five-slot receiver on Green, the Yellow receiver on the Yellow Tower. Note: finishing a puzzle doesn't submit its reward — someone has to pick the reward up and push it into a slot. The blank key drops only once every slot is lit.

  2. 02

    Find the first key cutter

    Once you have the blank key, the first cutter is usually right at the tower's base or just in front of its door. For the Red Tower it's the small slot at the entrance; for Green it's by the chairlift station beside the tower; for Yellow it's outside the tower door. Insert the key to start the cutting animation.

  3. 03

    Follow the arrow to the next cutter

    After each cut, an arrow on top of the cutter points to the next one. But the arrow gives a direction, not an elevation — the next cutter can be above, below, or hidden behind a sightline. Let the cut finish, read the arrow, then pull the key. Don't hunt blindly.

  4. 04

    Cut in order until the key is fully shaped

    Cutters must be done in sequence — skip one and the next won't accept your key. After all five, the key switches from blank to visibly shaped. The Red Tower's finished key slots into the terminal above the blue tunnel with red stairs for the Map Room; Green's goes into the chairlift station beside the tower; Yellow's goes into the Yellow lock for the underground tunnel.

  5. 05

    Use the finished key on the right lock

    Only a fully shaped key will open anything. Red: slot it into the blue-tunnel terminal (Map Room, portable map + compass, Big View). Green: insert at the chairlift station, wait for the chairs to circulate and board a moving seat for Big Sit. Yellow: insert into the Yellow lock and step through the threshold for the island tunnel. An uncut or partially-cut key won't seat.

Why it won't work

Why won't my key go into the lock/door?

It's almost certainly not fully cut. The key only opens things after all five cuts, in order. A blank or partially-cut key won't seat, so don't head straight to the door with one.

A cutter won't accept my key?

Cutters work in sequence. You likely skipped the previous one — go back to where the last arrow pointed, complete the missed cuts, and the later machine will accept it.

The key never dropped?

Your rewards aren't all placed, and it has to be done in person. Finishing a puzzle doesn't auto-submit the reward; check every slot has a physical item. If even one is missing, the key won't appear.

Can't find the next cutter?

Don't search blindly — read the arrow/light marker on the cutter you just used. It only gives a direction, not an elevation, so scan high and low ground nearby; the next one can be above, below, or behind.

The key carrier fell and the key is gone?

The ground is uneven and the key can slide much farther than the player. Stop, confirm who still has it and where it fell, retrieve it from the slope, then continue — don't chase the next cutter first.

I cut everything but the lock still won't open?

Check two things: is the key fully shaped, and is it the right lock? The Green key goes into the chairlift station beside the tower (not the inactive Train Station); the Yellow key opens the Yellow lock and counts when you cross the threshold; the Blue key goes into the station control panel.

Who should hold the key?

Keep one permanent carrier from blank key to unlock, with a second player on light and arrow reading, and the rest following. Don't toss the key between players, and don't assume a key that seats in the next machine already received the previous cut.

I finished the puzzles but one slot is still empty?

A reward wasn't carried back or was left somewhere. Tally what's in the receiver, what's in players' hands, and what's cached mid-route — the missing one is usually beside a puzzle, in the cache, or in a backpack.

Do these things and the key won't give you trouble

Keep one permanent key carrier

The key is the single most important thing to watch across the whole run. Give it to one person and don't swap it from blank key to unlock; everyone else handles light, arrows, and keeping clear so the key doesn't vanish into a crowd.

Pull after the cut, after the arrow

At each cutter, let the cutting animation fully finish and read the arrow on top before removing the key. The arrow gives direction but not elevation — the next cutter can be above, below, or out of sight, so scan high and low ground first.

Recover the key before chasing the next arrow

The ground is rough; if the carrier falls the key can slide farther downhill. Stop, confirm who still holds it and where it went, retrieve it, then continue.

Solving a puzzle is not submitting a reward

A lot of teams get stuck with 'we solved it but a slot is still empty.' Finishing a puzzle doesn't submit its reward — someone has to carry the physical Head to a receiver. Before leaving, count what's in the slots, what's in hands, and what's cached.

Don't mistake transport for the key's target

The inactive Train Station on Green is just a Head cache, not the key's destination — the finished Green key goes into the chairlift station beside the tower. The Yellow tunnel is only a shortcut under the island; opening it gets you to the Blue side, but you still have to collect the Blue Tower's own five Heads.
