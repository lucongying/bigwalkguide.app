Unlock the Map Room and read coordinates

The map is your only navigation tool in Big Walk, and it's locked inside the Map Room behind the Red Tower. Here's where the Red Tower is, how to solve all 5 puzzles, how to open the room, and how to actually read the coordinates. Once it's open you get the only paper map in the game plus a compass, and with a GPS on top you stop guessing and navigate by true coordinates. Stepping into the Map Room for the first time also unlocks the Big View achievement.

Before you start

  • Crossed the red drawbridge (starting area's 4 puzzles + cut the key)
  • At least 2 players — every puzzle needs coordination
  • Know the key-cutting mechanic (keys must be cut before opening the blue door)
  • More players just add a few more switches or raise a target number — the game scales for 2/3/4 players, not the logic

How to solve it

  1. 01

    Find the Red Tower (3670, 1400)

    After the drawbridge, turn left at the first fork into the red area with two round platforms, then look for a path leading up the mountain. The Red Tower is the upside-down cone on the summit. If it's hard to pin down in daylight, walk to the inactive train station, keep the red beacon blinking above the trees in front of you and follow beside the rails instead of climbing straight up the face — it works its way to the tower base. The red key cutter at the bottom is an easy landmark.

  2. 02

    Solve 5 puzzles for 5 red gourds

    The five puzzles are spread around the Red Tower (beach, forest, train station, summit). Each one rewards a red gourd collectible. The pieces aren't tied to one fixed set — there are more than five solvable puzzles in the area, so pick any five (or the five easiest to reach) to fill the count. The five below are the ones closest to the tower, easiest to do, and most commonly documented.

  3. 03

    Place the gourds at the top for the golden key

    The top of the Red Tower holds a console with five slots. Slot all five red gourds in; once the fifth lights up, the golden key flies out of the back of the panel. Missing even one means no key.

  4. 04

    Cut the golden key

    What you have is a rough blank. Follow the arrows through all five cutters, starting at the one by the tower door. Order and spot: ① the one by the tower gate → ② one beside a few rocks along the path, heading downhill → ③ one near a tree → ④ one at the edge of the entrance to the red area with the two round platforms → ⑤ the last one to the left of the Map Room staircase, just before the door. Each cutter points to the next.

  5. 05

    Open the blue door to the Map Room (3560, 1425)

    Insert the shaped key into the large blue door with the red staircase. Inside, grab the foldable map and compass from the table — the only navigation map in the game and the basis for everything that follows. Stepping into the room for the first time also unlocks the Big View achievement. For how the 3D terrain map, GPS and the rest differ, see the reading section below.

The 5 Red Tower puzzles (1 red gourd each — pick any 5 nearby)

  • Silent Room3760, 1430
    Green building at the bottom of the tower path (3760,1430). One player goes into the soundproofed control-panel room and gets locked in; a stone on the panel lights up. Point through the glass to the matching button on the outside wall so a teammate presses it. Each hit fills another chunk of the long black screen — repeat about 10 times for the gourd. The locked-in player can't see the outside panel, so it's all verbal.
  • Beach Maze3840, 1350
    Southwest of the tower on the beach (3840,1350). Split up: the wind-up timer sits on the left of the maze and its holder on the right; a talking window sits around the corner from both endpoints. The player by the timer hands it over, the other slots it on their side — the trick is not letting it stop en route.
  • Pass the Ball3740, 1620
    Southeast of the tower across the ravine and into the forest (3740,1620), on a dark purple-blue platform with the ball on a spike. The holder can't move — to shift the ball, stand in front of them and let them hand it to you, then relay (more players means a human chain; grab it from as far as you can each time). Get it from the start platform up to the goal platform on the hillside; a lantern by the end helps as daylight fades.
  • Basketball Hoop3830, 1560
    Same forest as Pass the Ball (3830,1560). Throw a lantern/glowing ball through the overhead hoop to fill the meter — about 8 baskets in total with two players. Sorting out the best angle first makes it much faster.
  • Tomato Cannon3880, 1520
    Visible from the top of the Red Tower, overlooking a small train station (3880,1520). One player trips the launcher on the left side of the yellow platform and a tomato-shaped egg timer flies down into the field. The other picks it up and throws it back to the player on the platform, who drops it into the hole of the green pedestal on the right. Be fast: head down and get positioned before it fires.

The Map Room's tools + how to read coordinates

There are two different maps

One is a big room-sized 3D terrain map (the physical island model) where flags mark what's done and bare poles mark what isn't. There's a third state to read from the item itself: if a puzzle's reward stands upright but has no flag, that challenge still needs finishing; if it lies flat, you've collected it but haven't submitted it to a slot yet. In front of it you can also read all island landmarks, tower positions, transport routes, completed challenge sites, and backpack and radio coordinates at a glance. The other is a foldable paper map you can zoom into specific sections (press the interact button) and sketch zones onto — used to find points by coordinates, but it never shows your live position. There's no other built-in map in the game.

Read the first number left/right, the second top/bottom

The numbers along the map's left/right edges match the first number of a coordinate; the top/bottom edges match the second. Read the first number left-to-right and the second top-to-bottom, then find the crossing.

Treat gridlines as ending in 00

For (3420, 1250): the first number 3420 sits between the 34 and 35 lines on the sides, and 1250 sits between the 12 and 13 lines on top/bottom.

Locate left/right first, then top/bottom

Pin the first number left-to-right, then the second top-to-bottom — where the two bands cross is your target. The closer a coordinate sits to a line, the closer that point is to the line.

The map won't show you → grab the GPS

The foldable map never tells you where you're standing. Inside a red square structure below the Green Tower near the chairlift there's a GPS tracker that shows your real-time coordinates in numbers. Match that readout against your target coordinate and you'll pin down both position and heading fast — and solve a couple of coordinate puzzles while you're at it.

The compass handles direction, for callouts

The compass only reports N/E/S/W and shows no terrain. Its job is calling directions to teammates: 'north of the Green Tower', 'follow the coastline south'. When it's too dark to read a map, a compass plus walkie-talkie still lines everyone up. The shelf in the Map Room holds the compass plus three laser pointers.

The foldable map needs a backpack

The handheld map can be stored in a backpack or a side satchel. Carry space is tight in Big Walk, so the usual move is to keep it stored somewhere safe and pull it out only when you need to solve a coordinate puzzle or get your bearings.

Pick one navigation tool, not all three

The portable map, compass and laser pointers solve different problems. Take the map when the next route crosses unfamiliar ground, uses coordinates, or combines several objectives. Take the compass when you already know the destination but need to hold one direction across open terrain. Take a laser pointer when one player can see a distant platform or tower the other can't. When both players need walkie-talkies, light is essential, or the next objective follows a visible beacon, leave the navigation tools behind — carrying capacity matters more than having every tool.

Why it won't work

Can't find the Red Tower?

Head uphill from the Map Room, or look for the upside-down cone shape. Turn left at the first fork into the red area, then find the mountain path. At night, the red beacon blinking above the trees is the most reliable cue — it rises above the inactive train station.

The golden key didn't drop?

Your five red gourds aren't all placed. Check every slot at the tower top — even one empty stops the key.

The key won't fit the blue door?

It's not cut yet. The Red Tower key has five cutters, starting by the tower door — finish them all before opening the door.

All five gourds are in, so why isn't the Map Room open?

Five gourds only produce a blank key — they don't open the room on their own. You still have to run that key through all five cutters to shape it before it fits the lock on the Map Room door.

Are the puzzles harder with more players?

No. More people just add a few switches to press or raise a target count. The game only scales for 2/3/4 players — the solution logic and steps are identical.

Read the coordinates backwards?

Remember: read the first number left/right, then the second number top/bottom. Don't treat 4166 as top/bottom.

Why isn't my position on the foldable map?

The paper map deliberately never shows your live spot — that's how it differs from the 3D terrain map and the GPS. To see your own coordinates, grab the GPS below the Green Tower near the chairlift; the paper map is for checking terrain and pinning your target.

What's the difference between the two maps?

The big 3D terrain map uses flags for what's done and bare poles for what isn't, and reads the in-between state off the item itself (upright without a flag = not yet finished; laid flat = collected but not submitted). The foldable paper map zooms and lets you sketch zones for pinning points by coordinates, but won't show your live position.

Where do I get the GPS?

In a red square structure below the Green Tower near the chairlift. It shows your current coordinates in real time, which pins you down fast against a target and even solves a few coordinate puzzles.

Too dark to read the coordinates?

Night is genuinely dark and the grid on the paper map is hard to read. Light it up with a lantern, a torch or one of the big glowing orbs (there's a torch in the Map Room too). If it's truly black, set a rough bearing with the compass first or just wait for daybreak. To trace your way back from the station/Map Room side, follow the red beacon blinking above the trees.
